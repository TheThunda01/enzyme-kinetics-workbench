# Assumptions and their consequences

The estimates describe one historical table under a two-parameter mean curve. They are not estimates of a treatment effect or a fully characterized enzyme mechanism.

The register separates evidence about the input from choices made for this analysis. “Checked” means only the stated check passed; it does not establish experimental validity.

| Assumption | Why it is used and what supports it | Check and consequence if it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The pinned table is the intended input. | The R Core Team’s source supplies the recorded units, values and labels. Freezing the commit keeps this analysis reproducible. | Checked: lengths, hashes and independent literal-to-CSV conversion. A passing check cannot recover undocumented assay history. |
| Rows retain their original order and values. | No averaging, outlier removal or relabeling is needed for the stated descriptive comparison. | Checked: exact conversion and 23 retained row IDs. Altering rows would define a new analysis. |
| The two labels may be described separately, not treated as independent replicated treatment groups. | The official documentation describes one experiment per label and lacks experimental-unit IDs. | The design limitation is documented, not repairable by fitting. A causal treatment interpretation would exceed the evidence. |
| The mean rate has the shape `V*S/(K+S)` with positive V and K. | This is the R example’s model and a deliberately simple saturating description. Briggs and Haldane (1925) and Johnson and Goody (2011) explain a classical mechanistic setting; that setting has not been established for this table. | Residual plots and range sensitivity provide limited criticism. No comparison establishes absence of baseline, cooperativity, inhibition or other mechanisms. If the mean model is wrong, fitted parameters can be misleading. |
| Recorded concentration is treated as exact for fitting. | The table has no concentration-error estimates. An error-in-variables model would require additional information. This is a pragmatic assumption. | Untested. Concentration measurement error could bias parameters and invalidate interval calculations. |
| Rate errors are independent, additive, Gaussian and share a variance within each label. | These assumptions justify unweighted least squares and the unknown-variance likelihood used for the profiles; the variance need not be equal between labels. | Residual plots can reveal some problems but do not establish independence or normality in groups of 12 and 11. If false, parameter intervals and error summaries may be poorly calibrated. |
| A chi-square profile cutoff is a useful approximation. | The cutoff follows an asymptotic likelihood-ratio argument, not demonstrated finite-sample coverage. Raue et al. (2009) provide profiling context. | No coverage simulation or bootstrap validation is implemented. “95%” names the nominal procedure; it is not a verified coverage rate for this archive. |
| Broad positive bounds and nine starts are sufficient for these fits. | Bounds `[1e-9, 1e9]` support positive log-parameter search; they are computational limits, not biological priors. | Convergence, bound flags, noiseless recovery and an independent scalar route are checked. Neither route certifies global optimality for arbitrary data. |
| Two numerical routes should agree for the same objective. | The verifier eliminates V analytically and searches log K, independently of the main two-parameter fit. | All 16 saved fits are compared at declared tolerances. Agreement does not validate noise assumptions or mechanistic meaning. |
| The low-range threshold and six whole-level omissions are fixed sensitivity questions. | The threshold 0.11 ppm and leave-one-level procedure illustrate dependence on the available range; they are analysis choices, not literature-validated assay designs. | All levels and omitted row IDs are retained. Omitted-row errors are not independent external validation; selecting only favorable omissions would bias interpretation. |
| The hypothetical information comparison fixes V=K=1, unit variance and 12 observations per grid. | Equal budgets and scaling isolate how three dimensionless concentration ranges change local derivatives. This is a mathematical exercise; no noisy observations are generated. | Grids and Jacobian checks are explicit. Different noise, costs, coordinates or parameter values can change the ranking; no practical optimum is established. |
| The local inverse-information approximation is interpretable only near the selected parameters. | It linearizes the response using log-parameter derivatives, as an aid to diagnosis. | The matrix, conditioning and correlation are reported. Strong nonlinearity or weak directions can make this approximation uninformative; it is not a second validated confidence interval. |
| Pinned software and complete output folders support reproducibility. | Version and source records make the calculation inspectable. Output files can be replaced in the selected destination. | Known-parameter tests and saved-result comparisons are available. A failed/partial write must not be interpreted as a complete run; matching hashes do not authenticate authorship. |

An initial-rate interpretation belongs to the archive’s documentation. The missing substrate identity, enzyme amount, calibration and detailed protocol prevent confirmation of the classical mechanistic assumptions or calculation of a molar binding affinity or turnover number.
